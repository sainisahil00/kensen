Five thousand years in steel

From arsenic-copper blades to folded steel, the sword changed with every metal and every battlefield.

c. 3300 BC

The earliest known swords, made of arsenic-copper, are buried in a tomb complex at Arslantepe in eastern Anatolia.

c. 1200 BC

Ironworking spreads across the Mediterranean and Near East. Iron is harder to forge than bronze, but the ore is far easier to find.

3rd century BC

Rome adopts a short Iberian thrusting sword, the gladius, and builds an army around close formation fighting.

800–1000 AD

Frankish smiths mark blades with the name Ulfberht. Some use crucible steel, a material that would not be common in Europe for centuries.

1185–1333

In the Kamakura period, Japanese smiths refine the curved, differentially hardened blade that becomes the katana's ancestor.

1500s

Civilian dueling in Spain and Italy favours the long, thin rapier, and fencing treatises turn the sword into a discipline.

1876

Japan's Haitōrei edict bans carrying swords in public. The katana moves from the street to the museum and the dojo.
